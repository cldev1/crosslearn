Renaming PMs solves the week and hurts hiring; standardize titles

Source: Melissa Perri (@lissijean)
original post

What it claims

Calling your Product Managers something else may solve some issues in the short term, but it is going to prevent you from hiring great people in the long term. Anyone who says titles are not important is either trying to screw someone over or in a very privileged position. Salary bands are determined by seniority. Salary bands are determined by job titles and positions. Director of Product vs VP of Product salaries are huge differences — title discrepancy, not a style choice (external salary-band charts are a pointer to that gap; the specific numbers are not the claim, and Perri does not necessarily agree with them). Prodmgmt is so nebulous compared to other disciplines — too many titles and too much discrepancy. We need to standardize this. It will help with hiring. It will help with org design. It will help with job descriptions. And it will help people get paid what they are worth.

Why it matters for a PO

“Let’s call them Product Owners / value leads / mini-CEOs so the org chart fight goes away” is the short-term rename. It then breaks hiring (candidates cannot find the role), salary bands (title ≠ seniority), and pay. Director vs VP is not a synonym. A PO who will push to standardize the title to the market band — rather than invent a local name that hides the work — is protecting hiring and pay, not being precious about a label.

3 takeaways

  1. Renaming PMs can quiet a week and then block hiring; people who say titles do not matter are privileged or extracting.
  2. Salary bands follow seniority and titles; Director vs VP is a huge pay gap, not a style choice.
  3. Prodmgmt is too nebulous; standardize titles to help hiring, org design, JDs, and pay.

Practice this week

Write the current product-role titles on the train vs the market title a candidate would search. If they do not match, do not add another local rename. Flag one band (PO vs PM vs Director) where the title is hiding seniority or pay, and take that to the hiring manager.
