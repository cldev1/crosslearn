Most A/B tests do nothing; swing for the fence, don’t treat α as FPR

Source: Teresa Torres (@ttorres)
original post

What it claims

Torres highlights takeaways from a paper on false discovery in A/B testing (ron-berman.com/papers/fdr.pdf); if the statistics are over your head, jump to the conclusion. Using Optimizely data, 70% of A/B tests had 0 effect — if most of your A/B tests don’t work, you aren’t alone. Thinking of your confidence value (e.g. 5% confidence) as your false positive rate is wrong; your false positive rate is likely much higher. If you aren’t running your A/B tests multiple times (the same exact test more than once), you are likely experiencing a lot of false positives; the paper goes into more specifics on how to optimize these repeat tests. Most of us are testing changes that are far too small to have any effect, regardless of sample size or confidence level. If you want to get value out of A/B testing, you have to test, as the authors call them, “swing for the fence” ideas. Torres has long been critical of A/B testing as the primary means for discovery: you get feedback way too late (after all the implementation work), and if you don’t understand the statistics it can send you down the wrong path quickly. However, A/B testing can be a fantastic measurement tool — but you need stats folks to help you get it right and a really robust testing architecture. Today’s A/B test tools aren’t doing enough to help us get it right.

Why it matters for a PO

A PO who treats every copy/color tweak as a “test” will mostly measure noise, then ship false winners. Discovery belongs earlier than the implemented variant. Use A/B as measurement on large, fence-swinging bets, with repeats and stats help — not as the way the trio “learns” after the code is already written.

3 takeaways

  1. ~70% of A/B tests in Optimizely data had zero effect; tiny changes stay tiny regardless of n or “95%.”
  2. Your 5% significance is not your false-positive rate; unreplicated tests inflate false winners.
  3. A/B is a late measurement tool, not primary discovery; swing-for-the-fence ideas, plus stats and architecture, are what make it valuable.

Practice this week

List in-flight or planned A/B tests. Kill or skip any that are too small to matter. For one remaining bet, write the discovery you will do before implementation, and whether you will replicate the same test rather than trust a single “significant” run.
